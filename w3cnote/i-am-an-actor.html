Why do I feel more sorrow watching Stephen Chow's movies as I get older?

Category Miscellaneous

The reason in the end: we have all become the kind of people we once despised

Think about this sentence, sometimes it really makes me feel sad. I wonder if this sentence can apply to you... Once, I had no plans for the future, just muddled along, but I never thought about being a salesperson... I used to think sales was this and that, thinking no matter how unsuccessful I was, I wouldn't become a salesperson, right?! And now, I am a salesperson, and I increasingly feel that sales is a good career... It's terrifying to think about it - where is my original intention? Today is the weekend, let's read something~

That is because in Stephen Chow's movies, there is a basic core, and this spiritual core has never changed amid the changes in the movies' outer forms. This has to start from an earlier, completely unrelated matter.

After Bo X某某 was put on trial, a sentence spread widely online, roughly: After Bo X某某 married his second wife, he began to accept bribes and commit corruption, and finally stepped into the abyss step by step. However, in court, his second wife and he accused each other, and his second son did not come from the US to see him; only his ex-wife and eldest son attended the court.

First of all, I won't argue whether this statement is accurate. But there were so many people online who felt the same way, which startled me. Because in some respects I agree with divorce or remarriage; after all, pursuing happiness can sometimes be a very personal matter. On reflection, I understand that this is the principle that Chinese people have worshipped since ancient times - the new is not as good as the old. In other words, it isPeople should stay true to their original self and never forget their original intention.

In short, the above example is an act of spreading information wrapped in the outer garment of good and evil being rewarded, under the spiritual core of staying true to one's original self.

Back to Stephen Chow's personally directed movies. If you recall carefully, in fact, in each movie there is such a core that supports the entire movie, and even guides the plot of the movie.

Starting from "The King of Comedy", the first sign of the core's appearance is when Stephen Chow recites that classic line,"I am an actor."He deeply pursued being an actor. Since he got close to Karen Mok, the word "actor" began to deviate from him, until the final play-within-a-play when he found his original heart again. The last scene of the movie is exactly "Thunderstorm", which Stephen Chow had been painstakingly planning while he was still unknown.

In "The God of Cookery", Stephen Chow was originally a god but was cast down to the mortal world. After a conspiracy, the God of Cookery lost his title, and after a series of battles to win it back, at the cookery competition, what he made was not any exquisite dish, but that bowl of super delicious mixed rice he had eaten when he was down and out. In the end, he realized: "As long as you have heart, everyone can be the God of Cookery."

This line appears twice in the movie, once earlier and once later.

After the success of "The God of Cookery", Stephen Chow launched two kung fu genre films, "Shaolin Soccer" and "Kung Fu Hustle".

In "Shaolin Soccer", this spiritual core became the main driving force of the movie's plot development. Ng Man-tat played Golden Right Foot, who after retiring had always been ashamed of having played match-fixing. And Stephen Chow's group of Shaolin brothers and sisters, after their master died, scattered to the city to work, and the past brotherhood was left in the past along with their martial arts.

Until Stephen Chow brought both sides together, they began a process of each person regaining their former self-esteem, their former martial arts, and their former friendship. When they were at a disadvantage in the final match, Vicki Zhao's character A Mei handed Stephen Chow that pair of patched old soccer shoes, and everything became clear.

Then came the great "Kung Fu Hustle" moment. The protagonist cherished a dream of helping the world in his childhood, but it was only to become a doctor or a martial arts master. He chose the latter, and because of his childhood experience of being bullied, he eventually degenerated into a small-time gangster.

When he encountered the Beast (Fire Cloud Evil God), both his martial arts and his kindness were forced out. Huang Shengyi represented all the beautiful things of the protagonist's childhood, like a smashed lollipop that could never be repaired. At the end of the movie, she and Stephen Chow ran hand in hand into the candy store.

Some people have said that Stephen Chow's movies contain the struggle of small fry. I don't fully agree. Stephen Chow's movies have always told us one thing:No matter what you forget, don't forget your initial pursuit.Stephen Chow used various roles to jointly interpret the theme of losing and regaining. He often wraps it in a happy ending, then shows it to us on the silver screen.When you laugh, Stephen Chow is nonsensical comedy; when you shed tears, Stephen Chow is again a sad adult fairy tale.

This is why Stephen Chow's movies, when watched again at an older age, make people more and more emotional.The reason, to put it in a sentimental way, is that we have all become the kind of people we once despised.
