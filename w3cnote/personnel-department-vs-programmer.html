Reading Resumes - HR Department VS Programmers

Category

During a programmer's interview process, you typically encounter people from two departments: the HR department that calls you, and the programmer who may become your future leader. So what's the difference between how HR and programmers review your resume?

Here's how HR departments read resumes

  • (+15 points) If the resume mentions skills matching the job position more than 5 times.
  • (+8 points) If the resume mentions skills matching the job position 3 to 5 times.
  • (+4 points) If the resume mentions skills matching the job position 1 to 2 times.
  • (+4 points) The cover letter mentions the recruiter.
  • (+2 points) The resume includes a cover letter.
  • (-10 points) Did not mention skills related to the job description.
  • (-15 points) Has not received an associate degree education.

Here's how programmers read resumes

  • (+15 points) Has written an operating system or compiler just for fun.
  • (+12 points) The resume was compiled with LaTeX.
  • (+11 points) Has contributed code to open-source software.
  • (+9 points) Wrote an operating system or compiler while in school.
  • (+8 points) Has a blog sharing technical knowledge.
  • (+8 points) President of a programming/robotics/engineering club.
  • (+7 points) Participant in programming/robotics/engineering competitions.
  • (+7 points) Has interned at Google and Microsoft.
  • (+6 points) Has written non-experimental programs using a dynamic language (Python/Perl/Ruby).
  • (+5 points) Knows 3 or more programming languages.
  • (+5 points) Previous work experience is very similar to the current position.
  • (+4 points) Has internship experience.
  • (+4 points) Has started a business or founded a company.
  • (+4 points) Has a personal website built with Rails, PHP, or ASP.NET.
  • (+3 points) Has an email address with a personal domain.
  • (+3 points) Has modified some programs written in a dynamic language (Python/Perl/Ruby).
  • (+2 points) Has a personal homepage.
  • (+1 point) Advanced degree, excellent academic performance, etc.
  • (+0 points) Has scholarships.
  • (+0 points) Worked at a fast-food restaurant.
  • (-0.5 points) Has a photo on Facebook where you look drunk.
  • (-1 point) Has a PhD.
  • (-2 points) Has a generic cover letter.
  • (-2 points) Says on the resume that you know Word/Excel.
  • (-2 points) There are spelling and grammar errors in the resume.
  • (-3 points) The resume's font is too small.
  • (-4 points) All programming experience is only from school.
  • (-4 points) Only knows one programming language.
  • (-6 points) The resume is more than three pages.
  • (-6 points) The resume contains some irrelevant things.
  • (-7 points) Has received certifications from some courses.
  • (-8 points) Low grades in relevant professional courses.
  • (-10 points) Lists Visual Basic first in the skills section.
  • (-12 points) Has had a shirtless photo on Facebook.
  • (-15 points) The resume uses both spaces and tabs for indentation.
