Don't do 7 things to boost productivity

Category

Being busy doesn't necessarily mean you are productive. To be productive, it's more about managing your energy rather than your time. You need to manage your life well. We need to learn to get the greatest returns with the least energy. By not doing the following 7 things, I cut my weekly working hours from 80 to 40, yet completed much more work.

1. High efficiency without overtime

The work schedule of 8 hours a day, 5 days a week was a discovery by Ford in 1926. Experiments showed that when daily working hours were cut from 10 to 8 and weekly working days from 6 to 5, productivity actually increased.

"Effect of Overtime on Construction Projects", published by The Business Roundtable in 1980, pointed out that whether in the short term or the long term, working more causes efficiency and productivity to decline:

If working more than 60 hours per week continues for more than two months, the cumulative effect of declining productivity will delay the completion date, while the same number of people working only 40 hours per week would finish even earlier.

U.S. military research shows that "losing one hour of sleep per day for a week causes cognitive impairment equivalent to a blood alcohol level of 0.1."

"The Secret World of Sleep" points out:

After staying up late, no matter how well you perform during the day, your mood will not be high. More importantly, the willingness to think ahead and take action, impulse control, optimism, empathy, emotional intelligence, etc., will also decline.

Therefore, ensuring adequate sleep is very important for maintaining high productivity.

Many famous people's experience is to take more naps during the day. Leonardo da Vinci, Napoleon, Edison, Eleanor Roosevelt, Gene Autry, Kennedy, Rockefeller, Churchill, Lyndon Johnson, Reagan, etc. all had the habit of napping.

2. Stop always saying "yes"

According to the 20/80 principle (Pareto principle), 20% of efforts produce 80% of results; conversely, 20% of results consume 80% of efforts. Therefore, we should focus our energy on things that produce 80% of results and give up the rest. This way, we can concentrate more time on the most important tasks. We should stop saying "yes" to low-output or even zero-result tasks.

The difference between successful people and very successful people is that the latter say "no" to almost everything.

— Warren Buffett

This raises a question: which things should we say "yes" to, and which should we say "no" to? If you can't think of which things are worth your time, try a simple split test. Track everything you do and optimize as much as possible.

Most of us tend to say "yes" too often, because it's much easier than refusing. No one wants to be the bad guy.

In 2012, a consumer magazine published a study in which researchers divided 120 people into 2 groups. One group was trained to say "I can't", and the other to say "I don't". The results were interesting:

Students who told themselves "I can't eat X" chose to eat chocolate candy 61% of the time, while those who told themselves "I don't eat X" succumbed to temptation only 36% of the time. Such a simple change in wording can significantly improve healthy food choices.

So, next time you need to avoid saying yes, just say "I don't."

Another technique to avoid unnecessary activities is the 20-second rule: give yourself an extra 20 seconds to consider things you shouldn't do.

3. Don't do everything yourself

I once managed a very large community and tried to do everything by myself, only to find I couldn't. I was exhausted, and in the end the community took over the self-management work. Surprisingly, everyone did better than I did. That's when I realized the power of community.

We must realize that it is important to seek help when needed. It is better for you to let people who do it better take over some of your work. This allows you to spend more time on your most important tasks. Don't waste time solving problems yourself; let experts help you.

Many times, even if friends can't help you, their companionship can make you more productive.

4. Don't be a perfectionist

Simon Sherry, a psychology professor at Dalhousie University, found in his research on perfectionism and productivity that perfectionism is a stumbling block to productivity:

Perfectionists need more time to complete tasks. Perfectionists therefore delay by waiting for the perfect moment. In business, if you wait for the perfect moment, it's already too late. Perfectionism often makes you miss the forest for the trees, because focusing too much on small things causes you to miss the big picture.

The best perfect moment is now.

5. Don't do repetitive work

According to a study by Tethys Solution, a 5-person team that spent 3%, 20%, 25%, 30%, and 70% of their time on repetitive work, after 2 months of productivity enhancement (introducing automation software), the time respectively dropped to 3%, 10%, 15%, 15%, and 10%.

Now whenever I need to repeat a task more than 5 times, I require myself to find a program to do it for me. It doesn't matter if you don't know programming; if you don't understand development, then buy a program.

People often forget that time is money. The reason they do things manually is usually because it's easier and requires no research. Handling 30 images seems fine, but what about 30,000? You can only rely on software. Remember, you need to spend money to make money, and time is your most valuable commodity.

6. Don't make decisions by guessing

If a website can be SEO'd, then so can life. Don't make decisions by wild guessing; support your decisions with data.

There are many studies in different fields available for reference. For example, did you know that people are most easily distracted at 4 p.m.? This is from a study led by Robert Matchock, an assistant professor at Penn State University. Even if you can't find the data you need, conducting a split test doesn't take too much time.

Keep asking yourself: how do you plan to measure and optimize everything you do?

7. Don't work all the time

Most people don't realize that if we focus too much on one thing, we box ourselves in and can't think outside the box. It's very important to step away from work and spend some time alone from time to time. According to "The Power of Solitude", time alone is very beneficial to the brain and spirit:

Harvard research shows that when someone believes they are experiencing something alone, their memory is more lasting and accurate. Another study shows that a certain amount of solitude can make people more empathetic. Although no one would question that excessive isolation early in life is unhealthy, a certain amount of solitude can improve adolescents' moods and grades.

It's important to leave some time for reflection. When we stop looking for solutions, they often come unexpectedly.

Of course, we can't become more productive overnight. Like everything in life, this requires effort. Sitting back and waiting won't bring change. We all need to understand our bodies better and find the best way to allocate our energy, so that we can have a more successful and happier life.
