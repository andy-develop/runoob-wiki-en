1. The Straight-Faced Bullshitting Art

When facing off with the product manager over requirements, you deadpan bullshit about features that are hard or unwanted to implement, using metaphysics and other tricks, leaving the PM dumbfounded on the spot and backing off.

2. Three-Stage Debugging Art

Stage 1: Brute-Force Debugging Art

When you can't find the problem, try commenting out the relevant code line by line until you find the problem.

Stage 2: The advanced version of "brute-force debugging": Binary-Search Debugging Art

The form is similar to the "binary search debugging" method. When you can't find the problem, try deleting half of the relevant code and compiling/running it. If the problem still exists, delete another half and compile/run again. Theoretically, this method takes O(logn) time (n = number of lines of code), and legend says it's slightly faster than the brute-force method.

Stage 3: Production Debugging Art

If the first two methods don't work, and you don't know whether the change is correct, just push it to production and try... If it doesn't work, roll back immediately!

11. The Pair Programming Art

Two like-minded programmers place their hands overlapping on the keyboard and program in a front-and-back body position. The technical term is: spooning (勺子).

It means staying unmoved after receiving the requirements, then starting to chat on WeChat, browse Weibo, and watch A/B sites. When the deadline approaches, you start implementing the requirements and finish a week's workload in one day or a few hours.
