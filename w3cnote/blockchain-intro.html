What is Blockchain?

From a literal perspective:Blockchain is a chain formed by linking together small blocks that record various information, similar to stacking bricks one by one. Once stacked, they cannot be removed. Each brick also has various information written on it, including: who stacked it, when it was stacked, what material the brick was made of, etc. You cannot modify this information either.

From a computer perspective:Blockchain is a special type of distributed database. A distributed database stores data information separately on each computer, and the stored information is consistent. If one or two computers break down, the information will not be lost, and you can still view it on other computers.

Blockchain is distributed, so it has no central point. Information is stored in all nodes that join the blockchain network, and the data on the nodes is synchronized. A node can be a server, laptop, mobile phone, etc.

What you need to know is that the data stored on these nodes is exactly the same.

Blockchain Characteristics

Decentralization:Because it is distributed storage, there is no central point. It can also be said that every node is a central point. In everyday life, this means no third-party systems are needed (banks, Alipay, real estate agents, etc., are all third parties).

Openness:The system data of blockchain is open and transparent, and everyone can participate. For example, when renting a house, you can know the previous rental information of the house and whether there have been any problems. Of course, some personal private information here is encrypted.

Autonomy:Blockchain adopts consensus-based specifications and protocols (such as a set of open and transparent algorithms), and then each node operates according to these specifications. This way, everything is completed by machines, leaving no room for human factors. This changes trust in "people" into trust in machines, and any human intervention has no effect.

Information Immutability:If information is stored in the blockchain, it is permanently saved and cannot be changed. As for the 51% attack, it is basically impossible to achieve.

Anonymity:There is no personal information on the blockchain because it is all encrypted, consisting of strings of numbers and letters. This way, there won't be situations where your ID information or phone numbers are resold.

How Blockchain Works

Let's use a transfer as an example:

Currently, our transfers are centralized. Banks are centralized ledgers. For example, account A has 400 yuan, and account B has 100 yuan.

When A wants to transfer 100 yuan to B, A must submit a transfer application through the bank. After the bank verifies and approves, 100 yuan is deducted from account A, and 100 yuan is added to account B.

After the calculation, account A has a balance of 300 yuan after deducting 100, and account B has a balance of 200 yuan after adding 100.

The steps of a transfer on the blockchain are: A wants to transfer 100 yuan to B. A announces this transfer information on the network, and everyone checks whether A's account has enough money to complete the transfer. If verification passes, everyone records this information on the blockchain in their own computers, and the information recorded by each person is synchronized and consistent. This way, A successfully transfers 100 yuan to B's account. You can see that the bank is not involved at all in this process.

What key technical points do I need to understand about blockchain?

Using Hash and asymmetric encryption to ensure data cannot be tampered with:

  • Hash: y = hash(x). Performing a hash operation on x gives y, which can hide the original information x, because you cannot calculate x from y, thereby achieving anonymity.

  • Asymmetric encryption: The public key and private key are a pair. If data is encrypted with the public key, it can only be decrypted with the corresponding private key; if data is encrypted with the private key, it can only be decrypted with the corresponding public key.

Consensus algorithm:Ensures data consistency between nodes.
