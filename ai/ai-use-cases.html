AI Application Scenarios

Many people's first impression of AI is a chatbot. Chatting with it is fun, but after a few conversations, they feel it's just so-so. That's because you treat it as a toy, not a tool.

The real value lies not in chatting with AI, but inusing AI to solve specific problems, such as writing emails, modifying code, organizing meeting minutes, and analyzing data — these are where AI can save you time.

The goal of this chapter is not to list all possible application scenarios, but to give you a thinking framework for finding AI use cases, and then let you experience how to put AI to work through a few typical scenarios.

AI's value lies not in what it can do, but in how much time it can save you. Before using AI each time, think clearly: What problem do I want to solve?

Writing and Content Creation

This is one of the most mature and easiest-to-use AI application scenarios. As long as text is involved, AI can almost always help.

Article Writing and Polishing

AI doesn't write a perfect article for you, but helps youquickly turn ideas into text。

Bug Debugging and Fixing

When encountering a bug, asking AI first has become a habit for many programmers.

Debugging Prompt Template

我遇到一个 Bug，请帮我分析可能的原因。

现象：[描述发生了什么，而不是你认为哪里错了]
期望结果：[应该发生什么]
实际结果：[实际发生了什么]

相关代码：
[相关的代码片段]

错误信息：
[完整的错误堆栈信息]

[你已经做过的排查]

Key principles:Tell the AI what happened, not what you think went wrong.。

If you directly say "I think it's a database problem," the AI may follow your line of thinking and miss the real cause.

AI-Assisted Design Workflow

Even if you don't know how to use professional design software, you can still create decent designs with AI.

Typical workflow:

  • 1. Describe what you want in text: A modern minimalist website homepage, with blue and white as the main colors, a top navigation bar, a Hero section in the middle for product introduction, three feature points below, and contact information at the bottom.

  • 2. Let AI generate the design draft: Use tools like Midjourney, DALL-E to generate a few images.

  • 3. Iterate and optimize: The layout of the first image is good, but the colors are too bright; make them darker and the font a bit larger.

  • 4. Convert to code: Give the final image to AI and let it implement it with HTML/CSS.

Customer Service and Q&A Systems

Using AI to answer customers' common questions is one of the easiest enterprise application scenarios to implement.

The Basic Logic of AI Customer Service

AI customer service doesn't need to "completely replace humans"; as long as it can answer 80% of common questions, it is already very valuable.

The simplest AI customer service workflow:

  • 1. Collect frequently asked questions: Organize the questions that customer service is often asked.

  • 2. Prepare standard answers: Write a clear and accurate answer for each question.

  • 3. Let AI match the questions: When a user asks a question, AI finds the most similar question in the FAQ database and returns the corresponding answer.

  • 4. Escalate complex issues to humans: If AI cannot find an answer or the user is not satisfied, automatically transfer to human customer service.

How to Find Your Own AI Use Cases

After seeing so many use cases, you still might not know: where should I start?

Here's a simple method to help you find AI use cases that work for you.

Step 1: Record Your Week

Get a piece of paper or a document and record what you do every day this week.

No need to be too detailed, just jot it down simply:

Monday: Wrote two emails, revised a report three times, attended two meetings, and put together a spreadsheet.

Tuesday: Wrote code, debugged bugs, wrote unit tests, and discussed requirements with the product manager.

Wednesday: Interviewed three people, organized interview notes, wrote a weekly report, and replied to customer questions.

...

Step 2: Identify Three Types of Tasks

In your records, mark the following three types of tasks:

  • Type 1: Tasks you hate doing—Things that give you a headache just thinking about them, but you have to do them anyway.

  • Type 2: Tasks you do repeatedly—Things you do once a week/month, with similar processes each time.

  • Type 3: Tasks you never have time for—Things you want to do but never get around to, like learning something new or organizing materials.

These three types of tasks are exactly where AI can help you the most.

Step 3: Start with the Smallest Thing

Don't start by thinking "I'll use AI to rebuild my workflow"—that's too complicated.

Find one thingthat's the smallest, clearest, and gives immediate resultsand start with that.

For example:

  • Not "have AI write my report for me," but "have AI help me make my report draft read more smoothly."

  • Not "have AI do data analysis for me," but "have AI explain what this Excel formula means."

  • Not "have AI write code for me," but "have AI add comments to this piece of code."

Step 4: Build Your Prompt Library

Save the prompts you've tried and found effective—a simple note-taking app is all you need. Copy and paste them each time you use them, then fine-tune based on the specific situation.
